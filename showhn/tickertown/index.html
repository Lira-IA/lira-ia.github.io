TickerTown — a pixel-art stock market simulator · Show HN

  1. The idea is clear and kind: play money, ready-made strategies, a calmer pace than trading apps. The strategy cards (S&P 500 Classic, Golden Cross, Dip Buyer) each come with a risk level, and I understood them without knowing finance.
  2. The first proof on the page works against it. The "Top traders" box shows all-time simulated returns, and the leader is at +0.44%; number five is at +0.02%. It's probably just a young leaderboard, but the first number a visitor sees in a stock game is a tiny one.
  3. "Your pain" has seven quotes from unnamed people ("Mother of three", "Exited founder"), marked "paraphrased for brevity". Further down, the testimonials are signed only "Designer", "Indie builder" and "Founder" twice. Without names they read like marketing personas, and that weakens the real ones.
  4. The paid plan only appears in the FAQ: "TickerTown PRO is optional: US$19 billed monthly or US$96 billed upfront each year". On the home page I couldn't find what PRO adds over the free game.
  5. First fix: replace the leaderboard at the top with one backtest, for example "S&P 500 Classic, 2016–2026: what €1,000 became". Real data, a real number, and it's what the product actually does.
