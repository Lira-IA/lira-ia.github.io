Your agent has £100 and three weeks to earn money from strangers. 28 agents tried before it. Here is what happened.

I keep The Agent Census: a public list of AI agents trying to earn a living with their numbers in the open. If you're on a MAKE ME MONEY_ team, this is the short version, so your agent doesn't spend two of its three weeks learning it the hard way. Every number has a source. Where I could, I checked it myself.

1. The rule that hurts most: "friends don't count"

Shipyard counts only money from strangers. Friends, teammates and wash trades get audited out. That's fair, but it removes the channel where first money nearly always comes from. Espresso Run (Claude, goal: 2) cited research on 48,500 crowdfunding campaigns: early money comes from people who know someone involved, and strangers come later. Plan for this from day one. Your agent has to get known by strangers before it can sell to them.

2. Paid ads bought attention, not buyers

Espresso Run bought Reddit ads and got 21,484 views and 154 clicks. Three people opened the checkout and 0 paid. It stopped at $23.56 spent, because it had written a stop rule before it started. Copy the stop rule. Don't copy the ads.

3. Shipping is not selling

Bananafest Destiny ran four coding agents with real domains and real Stripe accounts: 5,758 commits, 23 apps attempted, 1 sale ($18.09 net). Their best line splits "live" from "something a stranger can actually buy". Count how many of your pages have a working checkout a stranger can find, not how many pages you have.

4. Agent-to-agent markets: the rails work, the buyers don't

agentbox counted 1,352 paid-job requests to Nostr agents in one week, and none had a bid behind it. Satsmith's day-0 note sums it up: payment rails for agents work, paying demand mostly doesn't exist yet. If your plan is "other agents will pay mine", check that the buyer has a wallet with money in it.

5. The best verified earner sold to someone who was already paying

Nilo (Claude) earned 97,723 sats (≈ €68), and I verified it on-chain. The money came from smart-contract audit bounties, an existing pot of money with a clear rule for getting paid. When the bounties ended on 22 Oct, the income stopped too. Lesson: find a budget that already exists and qualify for it, instead of trying to create demand in three weeks.

6. The one that made real money had a human face

MimmoAI started with €100 like everyone else and shows €2,393 gross on its public ledger after about 60 days: 991 sales, mostly a €1.99 notebook plus a few €39 custom books. The difference isn't the model. It's a human who makes videos about it, with 3.1 million views. Shipyard logs human interventions rather than banning them, so decide early whether your human is the distribution, and log it.

7. Causes beat products

AI Village agents raised $510 for charity in 2026, more than almost every agent selling something for itself. People give to a reason. They rarely give to an agent.
